# Seller Revenue | Commercial Decision Support: overview

Which sellers should we help, what should we recommend, and when is a human conversation worth more than an automated intervention?

Version v1.0. Generated by `tools/build_overview.py` from the code, at each category's default settings, so the numbers match the app. Code: https://github.com/AstraErgo07/oncommerce-seller-revenue.

Portfolio project. Made by Ami Sata. A prototype for discussion, not production software.

## Purpose

A decision-support prototype for OnBuy (part of OnCommerce) seller revenue in climate control (Heaters, Fans, Dehumidifiers, Portable AC, Air Purifiers). For each seller: what they are worth to OnCommerce a month, one recommended next action (fix a sales leak, Boost, upgrade, retention or downgrade risk, or listing work), what it is worth after its cost, and whether a Seller Success call or an automated message is the better route. The calculations are plain Python rules. A person approves or overrides every recommendation, with a reason, and every decision is logged. An LLM is optional and only drafts message text.

## Audit: what was wrong and what changed

| Problem | Impact | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Call or message was judged on gross platform revenue over 12 months, with no OnCommerce margin and no seller churn. | Calls looked better value than they are. | Value is now platform revenue × 70% contribution margin × months still trading (9.9 of 12 at 3% churn) × success rate, minus the cost of the call or message. |
| The £120 call and £2 message costs were placeholders with no build-up. | The tipping point (£49.17) had no support. | Costs are built up from salary, productive hours and hours per call (£39.38), and from the journey build cost per seller plus sending (£0.80). All editable, all tagged Assumption. |
| Totals added every play, so a seller with three plays was counted three times and product plays overlapped with account plays. | Expected value and the human/automated split were overstated (203 plays, £5,933 a month expected). | One primary action per seller, plus an optional secondary. Only the primary is counted in totals and in the forecast. |
| The upgrade case counted the seller's extra sales at full margin, ignored commission on those sales, assumed a 5% (Partner) and 4% (Professional) sales lift with no evidence, and ignored the January 2028 price rise. | Upgrades were recommended to sellers who might lose money. | Seller gain = extra sales × (seller margin − commission) − extra fee. The lift defaults to 2%, is editable, and the upgrade is dropped if the seller is not better off. It is marked Needs validation if the tier or orders are estimated, the gain is thin, or the seller would lose money at the 2028 list price. |
| Retention counted the seller's whole monthly revenue as saved. | Retention value was overstated: it assumed every at-risk seller would leave without help, taking all their revenue. | Value at risk = revenue × 30% chance of losing the seller without help (Assumption); for a downgrade risk, only the difference between the two subscription prices. |
| Boost counted fees from sellers who would have boosted anyway, and could be suggested for duplicate pages, zero-sales listings or while a stock or listing problem was open. | Boost value was overstated and could be recommended before a fix. | Boost value is scaled by (1 − current Boost take-up); eligibility excludes out of stock, price problems, poor quality or duplicate pages, out of season, no sales and poor seller return; it must repay its own message; and it is deferred while the seller has a fix outstanding. |
| The overview said a seller appears on two category pages and ran one merged calculation, but the example seller names are per category. | Unrelated synthetic sellers could be merged. | The landing page sums per-category results and labels sellers as Seller (Category). |
| Missing delivery fees (blank, not zero) produced blank values for listing-fix actions when the engine was called without the data loader. | A blank value could drop out of totals unnoticed. | Missing numbers now count as 0 inside the engine as well as in the loader; covered by a test. |

Not changed, noted as limits: the badge-based unit floors are applied before seasonality is taken out, so off-season months can look slightly high for listings on a floor.

## Why call vs message?

Should someone phone this seller, or is an automated message enough? A call costs about £39.38 of staff time and works about 35% of the time. A message costs about £0.80 and works about 15% of the time. A call works 20 percentage points more often but costs £38.58 more, so it only pays when enough money is at stake. What counts is OnCommerce's contribution, not the seller's sales: each extra £1 of platform revenue a month is worth 70% as contribution, for about 9.9 months out of the next 12 (some sellers leave: 3% a month). So the tipping point is £38.58 ÷ (70% × 9.9 × 20%) ≈ £28 of platform revenue a month. Above it: call. Below it: send the message.

### What a call and a message cost (rough estimates, not OnCommerce data)

Call cost (assumption, a rough estimate): £45,000 fully loaded yearly cost of a Seller Success exec ÷ 1,600 productive hours a year = £28.12 an hour, × 1.4 hours per completed call (prep, attempts, the call, follow-up and CRM notes) = £39.38. Message cost (assumption, a rough estimate): £1,500 to build and maintain the journey ÷ 2,000 sellers reached = £0.75, + £0.05 to send = £0.80. Neither is OnCommerce data.

| Hours per completed call (prep, attempts, call, follow-up, CRM notes) | 1.4 | Assumption |

### The tipping point (break-even)

A Seller Success call is worth it when the platform revenue at stake a month is above (call cost − message cost) ÷ (OnCommerce margin × months still trading × (call success − message success)).

Months still trading: of the next 12 months a seller is expected to trade for 9.90, because 3% leave each month (0.97 + 0.97² + ... + 0.97¹²).

At the defaults: (£39.38 − £0.80) ÷ (70% × 9.90 × (35% − 15%)) = £27.83 a month.

Before this version the tipping point was £49.17: a £120 call and £2 message (unsupported placeholders) on gross revenue over 12 months, with no margin and no churn. The new costs alone on that old basis give £16.07. Counting contribution rather than revenue, and allowing for sellers who leave, raises it to £27.83.

## Default settings

All assumptions are in one `Settings` object passed to every calculation. Defaults (Heaters page; the settings hash for it is `ea8173b7b596`):

- Reviews: 1 in 50 buyers. Sales spread over the months since the first review where every review is listed, otherwise 24 months (at least 6). Halve units if the newest review is older than 12 months.
- Call success 35%, message success 15%; call cost £39.38, message cost £0.80 (built up in 'Why call vs message?'); value counted over 12 months.
- Upgrade sales lift 2% (Partner), 2% (Professional); seller margin 35%; chance an at-risk seller leaves without help 30%.
- OnCommerce margin 70%; seller churn 3% a month; upgrades 2% a month.
- Seller reviews to orders: 1 review per about 15.1 orders (6.6 seller reviews per 100 orders; median of the sellers showing both numbers, explained in worked example 2).
- Average order value £35; tenure 36 months; Partner if at least 10k lifetime orders and no badge.
- Boost: 25% of sellers on this page, level Plus, 30% of their sales boosted.

## Every Seller Revenue formula in plain words

- Est. units/month: how many items we think the listing sells in the current month.
- Avg selling price per unit (£): what a buyer pays for one item, including any delivery fee. Where several sellers offer the same product, it is the average across their prices, weighted by how many sales each is expected to win.
- Est. GMV/month: the value of those sales: units x average selling price. GMV means gross merchandise value, the total buyers pay.
- Season factor: how busy this month is compared with an average month for this kind of product. 1.0 is an average month, 2.0 is twice as busy, 0.3 is about a third.
- Revenue to OnCommerce: what the seller pays OnCommerce each month: subscription + commission + Boost fees.

Units for one listing, step by step:

1. Count the reviews on the listing.
2. About 1 in 50 buyers leaves a review (2%), so reviews x 50 = items sold to date.
3. Divide by the months the listing has been selling (time since the first review where all reviews are listed, otherwise 24 months). That gives sales in an average month.
4. If the newest review is more than 12 months old, halve it.
5. Multiply by the season factor for the current month.
6. OnBuy badges set a minimum: Bestseller 10, Hot 5, In demand 3 a month; a listing with no badge and no reviews counts as 0.5.
7. Out of stock, or a price that looks wrong: 0 this month.
8. Where several sellers offer the product, the recommended offer wins 80% of its sales and the others share the rest.
9. GMV = units x average selling price per unit (including delivery).
10. Commission = units x the larger of 15% of the delivered price and £0.25.

What a seller is worth to OnCommerce a month:

1. Lifetime orders: the order count in the OnBuy seller box (real, a minimum because of the +), or the seller's review count x about 15 (1 review per about 15 orders: the median orders per seller review among sellers that show both).
2. Orders a month = lifetime orders / 36 months.
3. Sales a month for the whole account = orders a month x £35 average order.
4. Tier: Professional where the badge is shown; otherwise Partner if at least 10k lifetime orders, else Standard. Subscription from the published price list.
5. Commission = 15% x sales.
6. Boost fees = share of sellers using Boost x share of their sales boosted (30%) x Boost fee level (Plus, 10%) x sales. The share using Boost is set per category (Heaters 25%, Fans 10% and so on).
7. Revenue = subscription + commission + Boost fees.
8. Lifetime value = revenue x 70% OnCommerce margin / 3% monthly churn (a seller stays about 33 months on average).
9. Forecast: each month 3% of sellers leave, 2% move up a tier, prices step up to list in January 2028, and accepted plays add their expected value over three months.

## The decision workflow

1. Load the category's listings (here, example data) and the published fees.
2. Work out units, average price, GMV and commission for each listing.
3. Work out each seller's revenue to OnCommerce and tier.
4. Run the action rules below; each candidate action gets the platform revenue at stake a month.
5. Value each action for a call and for a message: stake × OnCommerce margin × months still trading × success rate, minus the cost. The route with the higher net value wins.
6. Fix first: if a seller has a stock, price, delivery, duplicate-page or listing problem, Boost and upgrades are deferred. Upgrades the seller may not benefit from are marked Needs validation.
7. One primary action per seller (the highest net contribution not deferred), plus an optional secondary. Only the primary is counted in totals. Sellers are ranked by that net contribution, not by fee size.
8. Each action gets a rule-based review date: 14 days for a stock or price fix, 30 for Boost, delivery, duplicate pages, listing work and retention, 60 for an upgrade.
9. A draft message is written by rules, or by an LLM if one is set up. The LLM sees only the topic, seller, product, reason, listing facts and published fees, never the route, the decision or an estimated £ figure, and the page labels each draft "Drafted by rules" or "Drafted by LLM".
10. The category manager approves or overrides each action, with a reason for an override. Every decision goes into a decision log (timestamp, page, seller, product, play, at_stake, route, expected_mo, decision, reason, settings_hash) that can be downloaded as a CSV.

Play rules:

- Upgrade subscription: Suggest the next subscription tier only when the seller would be better off: extra sales from the tier's features (assumption: 2% for Partner, 2% for Professional; no public evidence, so cautious) × the seller's margin after commission (35% − 15%) must be more than the extra monthly fee. Marked 'Needs validation' if the tier or orders are estimated, the gain is thin, or the seller would lose money at the January 2028 list price. Deferred if the seller has a sales leak to fix first. At stake: the extra fee plus commission on the extra sales.
- Retention / downgrade risk: Flag a seller whose subscription is more than 5% of their estimated monthly sales, now or after the promotional prices end in January 2028, because they may cancel or downgrade. At stake: what would be lost (the whole account if they cancel, the tier difference if they downgrade) × the assumed 30% chance of losing it without help.
- Boost adoption: Suggest Boost only for an in-stock, correctly priced listing in its busy season (season factor at least 1.0) that scores at least 70/100 for listing quality, is not a duplicate page, is priced within 10% of the cheapest offer, has no Bestseller badge, where the Boost fee pays for itself for the seller and the platform's return covers the cost of asking. Deferred if the seller has a sales leak to fix first. At stake: Boost fees plus commission on the extra sales, × 75% (the share of sellers not already using Boost, whose Boost fees are already in the base revenue).
- Listing fix to unlock sales: Suggest a listing fix when a listing has no reviews yet or scores under 70/100 (missing brand, paid or slow delivery, low stock and similar). At stake: commission if it reached 2 sales a month, or 10% more commission.
- Leakage: out of stock in season: Flag a listing that is out of stock. At stake: the commission on the sales it would make this month.
- Leakage: duplicate product page: Flag a product listed on two separate pages. At stake: merging them is assumed to add 5% to its commission.
- Leakage: paid or slow delivery: Flag paid delivery or a delivery promise over 7 days. At stake: fixing it is assumed to add 10% to its commission.
- Leakage: price anomaly: Flag a price that looks wrong for the product, or an offer more than twice the cheapest offer on the same page. No £ is put on it; it is a check for the category manager.

Routing: Each action has a monthly platform revenue at stake. A Seller Success call is assumed to work 35% of the time and cost £39.38; an automated message works 15% of the time and costs £0.80. Value = £ at stake × 70% OnCommerce margin × 9.9 months still trading × success rate, minus the cost. The route that leaves more contribution wins: a call when about £28 a month or more is at stake.

## Limits

- The OnBuy data is synthetic. It copies the shape of a small sample found through search, which favours listings that rank well. It says nothing about any real seller.
- Units are inferred from reviews. Every £ figure scales with them.
- Season factors, success rates, the upgrade sales lift, the chance an at-risk seller leaves and the cost build-up for calls and messages are judgement, not data. None has been tested against OnBuy results.
- Fix first is applied per seller, so a small fix can hold back a larger upgrade; the upgrade still shows as a deferred secondary action.
- The badge-based unit floors are applied before seasonality is taken out, so off-season months can look slightly high for listings on a floor.
- This is a prototype for discussion, not production software.
- OnBuy's website terms restrict automated collection and reuse of its content, so the repository ships synthetic data only.
